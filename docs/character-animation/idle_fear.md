# Study 02 — IDLE_FEAR

**Dependency:** do not start until `IDLE_N` camera, base pose, identity and frame-production method are approved.
**Emotional read:** alert fear under control; the same person is trying to remain still.
**Not in this study:** walking, attack, panic run, color-only state change, scenery.

## Acting hypothesis

Fear changes timing and muscle tension, not the protagonist's identity. Begin from the approved normal idle and define a distinct but restrained base posture: shoulders held higher/inward, neck slightly withdrawn, elbows/forearms subtly protective, weight cautious. The breathing can be shallower and interrupted: a small inhale, brief catch/hold, then a controlled release. The loop must remain readable without trembling every body part every frame.

Use one small secondary reaction only if it improves the acting (for example, a brief head/shoulder attention shift) and return to the same loop pose. Do not make a startle event recur as ordinary idle. Do not represent fear through palette change alone.

## Frame plan: 8-frame prototype

1. Guarded fear-rest baseline.
2. Shallow inhale begins at upper torso.
3. Shoulders/neck tighten a little after chest.
4. Short caught breath/hold; arms stay protective.
5. Tension peaks subtly; no large flinch.
6. Release begins; torso softens before the shoulders.
7. Shoulder/arms follow the release with a small delay.
8. Return to the guarded baseline with a smooth loop join.

All eight frames need purposeful differences; no random pixel shake. The feet/contact pivot remains grounded unless the chosen fear acting explicitly transfers weight. If weight shifts, keep the foot plant and center-of-mass change anatomically plausible.

## Distinction from IDLE_NORMAL

Compare at equal scale side by side only during review:

- the same face, hair, body, outfit, palette and camera;
- normal idle is open/alert and controlled;
- fear idle has guarded compression, held tension and less regular breathing;
- do not recolor skin/clothes to signal emotion;
- do not enlarge eyes or exaggerate mouth into a caricature.

## Directions and output

After the state is approved, produce separate loops for `IDLE_FEAR_FRENTE`, `IDLE_FEAR_TRAS`, `IDLE_FEAR_ESQUERDA`, and `IDLE_FEAR_DIREITA`. Every direction remains seen from the same overhead/oblique camera. Each directory gets individual transparent PNGs; no four-direction contact sheet is an animation source.

## Failure conditions

- Fear is only a color swap.
- Constant full-body vibration, random arm movement, or a looping jump.
- Movement so small it vanishes at gameplay scale, or so large it reads as another action.
- Direction changes alter the camera or turn into a conventional front/side turnaround.
- Fear loop does not return to a guarded resting pose.

## Research basis

The delayed shoulder/arm follow-through is adapted from [AnimSchool's breathing breakdown](https://blog.animschool.edu/2024/11/15/breathing-life-into-your-animation/) with a pixel-art scale check. Emotional choices here are project hypotheses to validate visually, not facts established by that source.
